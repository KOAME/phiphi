How European Startups Are Putting AI to Work

A closer look at how early-stage companies are adopting AI assistants for internal research, knowledge management, and everyday decision-making.

October 2026 · Research Brief · 4 min read

AI is becoming part of everyday work

For early-stage companies, access to information can be as important as access to capital. Small teams often manage customer research, market intelligence, internal documentation, and operational decisions without dedicated specialist departments.

AI assistants offer new ways to manage these demands. They can summarise documents, organise research notes, compare information, and help teams navigate growing collections of internal and external knowledge.

Adoption at a glance

PhiPhi's October 2026 research scenario covers 69 early-stage startups across Europe. In this illustrative dataset, 35% use AI assistants for internal research at least once a week.

From search to knowledge workflows

The practical value of AI increasingly extends beyond producing text. Teams are exploring how assistants can help locate relevant information, connect internal documentation, and reduce the time spent moving between disconnected knowledge systems.

These workflows also introduce important questions. An assistant may retrieve useful information from a public website while missing relevant documents on another platform. The quality of its answers therefore depends partly on which sources it can access.

What comes next

For smaller companies, the next challenge is not simply adopting more AI tools. It is understanding where those tools are dependable, where human verification remains necessary, and how to make organisational knowledge accessible without losing control over it.

PhiPhi explores practical approaches to building information workflows that are transparent, adaptable, and useful for small teams.
